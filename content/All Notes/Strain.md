[[General and Numerical Relativity]]
### Preface to Strain
- From the [[The Gravitational Equivalence Principle]], we know that an object accelerating in a gravitational field will not detect any acceleration
	- this is because all parts of the apparatus will experience the same force, and thus no NET force is seen
- From [[The Gravitational Gradient]], we know that we can measure the change in acceleration that an object experiences over a distance, which comes out to be $g' \propto \frac{1}{r^3}$  
	- This relationship is that of the tidal field

### Understanding Strain
- Strain, mathematically, is taken to be 
$$h = \iint g' dt^2$$
	- note that $dt$ is being squared because it is two time integrals
- Since $g'$ is the difference in field (acceleration) between two points, the time integral will tell us the position difference that will be experienced by a two test masses in this radiative gravitational field
- this builds nicely to the definition of [[Weyl Tensor and Scalar]]
$$\psi_4 = \frac{\partial^2}{\partial t^2} (h_+ -ih_{\times})$$
- as we can see, the $\psi_4$ involves taking the derivative of $h$ two times, so in a way it spits the gravitational gradient right back out!
- this gravitational gradient is the difference in acceleration (field) experienced by two points close by as they are being streched and squeezed by the radiative gravitational field

### What is $h_+$ and $h_\times ?$
- These are components of the strain in two different directions of space time
- $h_+$ is the strain along the direction of propogation, $h_\times$ is the same but rotated 45 degrees
	- this rotation corresponds to a new polarization (orthogonal wave)
	- this is orthogonal because this is the world of tensors
