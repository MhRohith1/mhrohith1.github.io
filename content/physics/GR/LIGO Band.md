#### What do all the different things mean in the above picture?
- On the left is a frequency-amplitude plot of the noise experienced by the LIGO detectors in Hanford and Livingston
- The curve reaches a low at around $10^2$ Hz
- Any incoming signal that is above this noise curve can be perceived to be an event
- The blue thick line indicates event GW150914 and so on for the different colored time-domain sketches

#### Why are some things going on longer than others?
- Why is blue so much shorter and orange so much longer?
- We can see that a larger portion of the orange belt is in the range that is detectable by LIGO
	-  this means that LIGO has a better time reading the event here, and that there is more data incoming that LIGO can actually understand and discern.
- **Lower mass binaries merge at higher frequencies and spiral for longer, leading to larger waveforms**
	- this is the important note that is captured in the left plot
	- you may ask "but the amplitude grows so large, the plot only appears to show the amplitude decreasing from 30 Hz" - this is because the y-axis is strain/$\sqrt{\text{hertz}}$, so the larger the frequency, the more the amplitude actually shrinks because the frequency grows so much faster than the strain
