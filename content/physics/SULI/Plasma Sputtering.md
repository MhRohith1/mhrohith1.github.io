### Introduction
- The example that is discussed [DJ. Economou](https://pdf.sciencedirectassets.com/271603/1-s2.0-S0040609000X01083/1-s2.0-S0040609099010561/main.pdf) is focused on etching on the chip using Chlorine (Cl) gas instead of Argon (Ar) gas
	- With chlorine, there are chemical processes that occur on the surface of the chip when there is bombardment
	- in Argon gas chambers, all changes are driven exclusively by physical bombardment and not chemical reactions -- such a process is called sputtering
- "Plasma is generated by applying radio frequency power between a pair of parallel plates in a low pressure chamber"
	- Since we are supplying a RF power source into the system, the electrons in the latent gas oscillate at rapid speeds, causing them to be removed from their parent molecules, creating an ionized gas (plasma)
	- This generates ions ($\text{Cl}_{2}^{+},\,\,\text{Ar}^{+}$ and radicals $\text{Cl}$)
- By the RF, there is an electric field generated (since the gas is surrounded by capacitors) and the ions bombard the silicon surface
	- Similarly, the radicals also bombard the silicon surface
- The bombardment process generates $\text{SiCl}_{4}$, which is desorbed by the wafer and is moved away by the gas
	- In our case, argon is noble and does not react with the silicon. It is pure momentum transfer that causes etching within the silicon wafer
### Definitions
- *High etch rate*: literally how fast the argon molecules etch onto the wafer: this can be measured in $\mu \text{m} /\text{sec}$ or even $\frac{\text{wafers}}{\text{h}}$
- *Uniformity*: refers to how certain regions of the wafer are etched differently than certain other wafers
	- So for example, a highly uniform etching process would entail that every part of the wafer is etched similar to every other part
	- So having a very normal IADF and uniform flux would show a uniform etching process
- *Selectivity*: refers to the etch rate of the material you want to etch compared to the etch rate of the material you don't want to etch
- *anistropy*: describes the directionality of the etch. a highly anistropic etching process is one that cuts deep (vertically) into the chip without etching much horizontally
- reactor design and operating parameters (such as the pressure and voltage) play an important role in ensuring that the etching is done in a desirable way

### The sheath
- the sheath is a thin layer of gas directly above the chip that is formed as a result of the RF power supply
- the electric field within the sheath is 100 times stronger than that of the bulk plasma, so that any ions in this area are greatly accelerated and bombard the wafer on the surface normal (the normality arises because the sheath is so thin compared to the wafer width)

### The RF Power Source and the DC Bias
- based on [plasma.com](https://www.plasma.com/en/plasma-technology-glossary/sputtering/) and [CM Melliar Smith](https://watermark02.silverchair.com/1008_1_online.pdf?)
- There are two electrodes, and there is an RF power source connected to one of the electrodes
	- RF stands for Radio frequency
	- This power source is an AC power source that oscillates with frequency 13.5 MHz
- This connection is "capacitively decoupled" - there is a capacitor placed between the power source and the electrode
- The RF power source powers the electrode and causes an electric field to emerge in the gas chamber. This field oscillates, causing rapid acceleration of the electrons and ionizing the gas
	- The electric field attracts electrons and causes the electrons to collide with the electrode connected to the RF power source
	- however these electrons are unable to escape because of the decoupling, so there is a buildup of negative charge on the surface of the electrode (this surface becomes a cathode)
	- the ions do not follow the electrons and remain relatively at rest because they are too heavy to be greatly accelerated over a field that oscillates at 13.5 MHz
- This negative charge establishes the cathode at a negative potential, permanently forming the DC bias
- It is this build up of negative potential begins to attract ions in the ionized gas
	- the ionized gas is much heavier than the electrons, and has enough energy to sputter / etch the desired pattern on the surface of the wafer that's been attached to the cathode

### Plasma Etching vs Deposition
- there is a difference between plasma etching and deposition in terms of what role the ion bombardment plays
