### Introduction
- based on [DJ. Economou](https://pdf.sciencedirectassets.com/271603/1-s2.0-S0040609000X01083/1-s2.0-S0040609099010561/main.pdf), [insert other sources here]
- Usually, in plasma pattern transfer processes (which is the process of creating the desired pattern on the chip), doing just sputtering is often not sufficient
- so we add reactive compounds like $\text{Cl}_{2}, \text{CF}_{4}$ that can interact with the silicon wafer on the substrate and cause chemical reactions to create anisotropic etches (or etches that we desire)
	- chemical reactions on the surface of the substrate are absolutely necessary to ensure that the etching / deposition process is good
	- pure physical sputtering etches away the material very uniformly: even parts of the wafer you want to preserve or parts of the mask which hide material you want to protect
	- physical sputtering is also incredibly damaging to the wafer as it is a violent collision of high energy ions on to the surface of the silicon wafer, which ruins the crystal lattice structure
